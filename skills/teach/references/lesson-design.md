# Lesson and practice design

Use this guide when creating a lesson, practice activity, review, quiz, or learning record.

## Pick one outcome

Choose the next outcome from the mission, current milestone, baseline, learning records, and time since prior practice. A lesson should leave the learner able to do one relevant thing they could not reliably do before.

Estimate the zone of proximal development from evidence. If the baseline is thin, use a small diagnostic prompt or task before teaching. Avoid quizzing for ceremony; assess only what changes the next teaching decision.

## Separate acquisition from retention

When introducing knowledge, reduce unnecessary difficulty. Define required terms, connect them to something the learner knows, show one worked example, and cite the source.

When building skill or retention, add desirable difficulty:

- retrieval before re-reading;
- spaced review of material learned earlier;
- interleaving of related skills after each can be attempted separately;
- a realistic task with quick, specific feedback;
- comparison of a near miss with a correct result.

Do not introduce new material merely because another numbered lesson is possible. Review, consolidation, real practice, or course completion may be the right next move.

## HTML lesson contract

A lesson should:

- fit in one sitting and state one practical outcome;
- use clean, readable, printable HTML;
- link the shared workspace stylesheet and existing reusable components;
- give only the knowledge needed for the practice;
- include a worked example when useful;
- require an action, retrieval, or decision from the learner;
- provide immediate feedback or a clear way to obtain it;
- cite factual claims and recommend one strong primary source;
- link relevant lessons and reference documents with relative paths;
- invite focused follow-up questions without padding the lesson.

For multiple-choice questions, do not leak the answer through length, grammar, detail, formatting, or a fixed position. Prefer a workspace component that shuffles options at render time while preserving answer identity. Validate the component before relying on it.

## Evidence of learning

Exposure, completion, confidence, and recognition do not prove learning. Look for recall after delay, correct transfer to a new example, successful execution, explanation in the learner's own words, or a real artifact that meets the stated criterion.

Record only decision-relevant progress. Add understood terms to a glossary after the learner can use them correctly, not when they first appear in a lesson.

## Milestones and ending

At a milestone, compare evidence with `MISSION.md`:

- If a gap is foundational, teach or practice it next.
- If performance is fluent but fragile, schedule retrieval after a delay.
- If the learner can transfer the skill, move to realistic practice.
- If the end condition is met, say so and propose either stopping or a lightweight maintenance plan.
- If the mission no longer matters, confirm whether to revise or end it.

The course is complete when the agreed end condition is demonstrated, not when every available topic has a lesson.
